Gia đình · the family

Three generations, one tiny kitchen

Quán means a small local eatery. Ours began in Grandma's front room, feeding the neighbours and the odd lost tourist. These days Mum runs the stoves, Nam runs the front, and the recipes still live in Grandma's head.

There's no head office and no franchise. When you eat here, you're eating at our house.

“Fish sauce is not optional.”

Ăn như người Sài Gòn · eat like a local

First time in Saigon? Start here.

Street food here runs on a few unwritten rules. We wrote them down for you.

How to eat phở

  1. Taste the broth first. Before you add anything. Mum is watching.

  2. Tear in the herbs. Thai basil, sawtooth herb, bean sprouts. Use as much as you like.

  3. Add lime and chili. Go slowly. You can always add more, but you can't take it back out.

  4. Chopsticks right, spoon left. Lift noodles onto the spoon, sip, then slurp. Slurping is a compliment.

Rules of the street

  • The tiny stools are normal. Everyone's knees end up near their chin. That's the fun.
  • Ice in your beer is normal too. Saigon is 33 °C most afternoons. You'll understand.
  • Packaged wet towels on the table may cost a few thousand đồng. Leave yours sealed if you don't want it.
  • Pay at the end. Say “tính tiền” and we'll bring the bill. Tipping isn't expected.
  • Clink and shout “dzô!” before you drink. Everyone at the table, every round.

Say it like a local

Rough Saigon-accent pronunciations. Nam will happily correct you.

  • Xin chào

    “sin chow”

    Hello

  • Cho tôi một…

    “chaw toy moht”

    One of those, please (then point)

  • Không cay

    “khome kai”

    Not spicy

  • Ngon quá!

    “ngon kwah”

    So delicious!

  • Một, hai, ba, dzô!

    “moht, hai, bah, yo!”

    One, two, three, cheers!

  • Không đá

    “khome dah”

    No ice

  • Tính tiền

    “tin tee-en”

    The bill, please

  • Cảm ơn

    “gahm uhn”

    Thank you
